slides-rs

Markdown in, a deck out: text, equations and figures that step in as you present them.

Press → to step, ← to step back, ↓ and ↑ to finish or restart a slide, and A to show every step at once.

Markdown

A slide is plain markdown, with bold, italic, code and links, split from the next one by a --- rule.

A quote, as the theme dresses it.

A # heading is the slide's title, a ## a subtitle, and each ### a column.

Lists step one item at a time

  • Each item is a step of its own
  • shown after the one before
    • nested items too
  • and numbered ones:
  1. first
  2. second

Equations step too

\begin{aligned} x^2 + 2x - 3 &= 0 \\ \htmlData{step=1}{(x + 1)^2 - 4} &\htmlData{step=1}{= 0} \\ \htmlData{step=2}{x + 1} &\htmlData{step=2}{= \pm 2} \\ \htmlData{step=3}{x} &\htmlData{step=3}{\in \{1, -3\}} \end{aligned}

\step{...} shows one step after the latest, and \also{...} along with it.

Rewriting an equation

\step: hidden, it keeps its space

\htmlData{step=..2}{x^2 + 2x - 3} \htmlData{step=2..3}{(x + 1)^2 - 4} \htmlData{step=3}{(x + 3)(x - 1)} = 0

\step*: hidden, it takes none

\htmlData{step=..2,collapse=true}{x^2 + 2x - 3} \htmlData{step=2..3,collapse=true}{(x + 1)^2 - 4} \htmlData{step=3,collapse=true}{(x + 3)(x - 1)} = 0

In a sentence

The roots of \htmlData{step=..2,collapse=true}{x^2 + 2x - 3}\htmlData{step=1..2,collapse=true}{\;\text{(factor it)}}\htmlData{step=2,collapse=true}{(x + 3)(x - 1)} are \htmlData{step=3}{-3} and \htmlData{step=3}{1}: the text after the polynomial moves along as it is rewritten, and the roots, hidden, keep their place.

A range is written as in Rust: [2..4] shows from step 2 up to 4, [..3] until 3, and [3..], or [3], from 3 on.

x^2 + 2x - 3 = \underbrace{x^2 + 2x + 1}_{\htmlData{step=4}{(x + 1)^2}} - 4

Code

Shown

A backtick fence is shown, as code:

print("Hello from the deck")

Run

A tilde fence is run, and shows what it outputs:

Hello from the deck

Its outputs are saved, and it runs again only once its code, or a file it reads, changes.

Rich outputs

Markdown, from a cell, with its items stepping:

  • one
  • two

Or HTML, as a table of data shows itself.

Figures

A figure's artists step as their gid says: step, also, or a range.

One curve at a time

Step(1, 2) is step=1..2, which next() moves along.

Motion

Along a line

Motion(dot, along=line), which moves as its column shows.

Turning with it

.rotate("auto") turns it along the path.

Easing and waiting

.timing(duration=...), with an easing=, or with the times t it is each fraction= of the path: before t[0], it waits.

Timed as plotted

With a time at each vertex, .timing(t) follows the line as it was plotted, and .starts(Step(1)) waits for the next step.

Lap after lap

.repeat(3, accumulate=True) goes on from where each lap ended, and .hold(False) goes back to the start once done.

Leg by leg

Each leg moves in a step of its own: stepping on before one ends takes it to its end, as the next begins.

The deck

Frontmatter

---
theme: dark           # light, paper, projector
aspect-ratio: 16:9    # 4:3, 1.6, none to fill
steps: false          # this file all at once
invert-figures: false # figures as drawn
fade-figures: false   # figures step at once
---

Other files join with <import-slide src="..." />.

Command line

  • slides-rs talk.md writes talk.html
  • --watch renders again on every change
  • --self-contained puts every file inside the page
  • --kernel picks xpython or python3
  • --clean removes outputs no cell uses anymore
  • The VS Code extension runs the cells as a notebook, stepping through its figures.
